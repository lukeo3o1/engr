# A coordinator, and workers that draft their own decisions

Five runs on the Dynamic UI slice (`2026-09-28-changeset-review.md`) ended in the
same place: whatever the guide said, an agent that both wrote the code and kept
the record recorded its decisions late or not at all. In the last of them, none
of 19 decisions reached the record in the session that made it. This run tried
a different shape rather than different wording: the session coordinates, and
subagents write the code.

## The setup

Arm M is arm K with three changes, at `148df66`:

- `skill/engr-delegate`, a sixth skill: define items in engr, hand each to a
  worker subagent with a packet built from engr output, have the worker commit,
  draft its decisions into the item's ChangeSet and return a fixed report, and
  record what it returns before delegating again.
- The hooks pass over a subagent's own tool calls (they arrive marked with
  `agent_id`, checked with a headless session first) and remind the coordinator
  when a subagent returns.
- One sentence of the prompt: "Work as a coordinator, as the `engr-delegate`
  skill describes: define the work as items in engr and hand each one to a
  subagent, rather than writing the code yourself."

Same task, sandbox base, Rule, model and turn caps (70, 70, 90) as K; Claude Code
2.1.284 against K's 2.1.283. The bar was written down before the first session
started (`M/preregistration.md` in the harness): all three of

1. at least half the decisions the blind audit finds are in engr by the end of
   the session that made them (K: 0 of 19);
2. at least 75% of the items state a check another agent could run (K: 0 of 8);
3. the sidecar matches the repository at one of the two real cuts (K: 0 of 2).

## The turn cap did not hold

The second session ran about 140 turns under a cap of 70, and was cut by hand
while a worker was running. A subagent run in the background restarts the main loop
when it finishes, and `--max-turns` counts each restart afresh: S2's stream has
four `result` events, of 31, 55, 53 and 40 turns. K's second session had the same
problem on a small scale — two background agents, eight turns past its cap. The
runner now counts the coordinator's turns across every restart and ends the
session's namespace past the cap; S3 finished on its own at about 50 and never
reached it.

So S2 did about twice the work a 70-turn cut would have allowed, which matters
for criterion 1 (more of the implementation happened in S2) and for the cost.

## What happened

- **S1** did no code. It surveyed the codebase through a subagent, settled the
  design, and admitted it as 21 Sections over three review rounds; staged three
  open questions; and wrote six items, one per module, none with a completion
  condition. The sidecar was right at the cut.
- **S2** delegated four items. Each worker committed its code and drafted its
  decisions into the item's ChangeSet from inside its own run — 9, 9 and 4 steps;
  the fourth was cut before it drafted any. The coordinator re-ran every check
  before closing an item, and admitted 20 of the 22 drafts. The sidecar at the
  cut said the fifth item was delegated and named its ChangeSet, and the working
  tree held that worker's uncommitted diff — exactly one row of the skill's
  resume table.
- **S3** found that row, finished the worker's diff, drafted two decisions from
  it, closed the last items and stopped at a Human candidate for the one
  decision whose review was exhausted.

The blind audit (`appendix-l-delegate-run-audit.md`, label Z) scored backlog 3,
work items 2, reasoning 3 and resume 3.

## Against the bar

| | K | M | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Decisions in engr within the session that made them | 0 of 19 | 41 of about 49 | met |
| Items with a check in engr | 0 of 8 | 0 of 6 | not met |
| Sidecar matching the repository at a cut | 0 of 2 | S1 yes; S2 yes but for one stale candidate code | met |

**M did not meet the bar**, which asked for all three.

The first row is the largest difference any change has made in these runs, and
not only because S1 recorded its design before writing code, which run three
also did. Twenty of the 41 are implementation decisions drafted by the worker
that made them, in the run that made them: in the five earlier runs, decisions
made while writing code were recorded a session later from the code, or never.

The second row failed in an instructive way. The workers were given detailed
checks — "Done when: `cargo build --workspace` and `cargo test --workspace` both
pass … `session.rs` exists with unit tests covering: open creates a Session
with an owner …" — in the packet, in the coordinator's words. The item text in
engr, capped at 160 characters, had none. Delegation forced the check to be
written; it was written where no successor looks, and in breach of the skill's
own rule that a packet is engr output.

## What it cost

US$24.2 against K's 10.8 (S1 5.5, S2 15.6, S3 2.3, probes 0.8). The audit puts
about 70% of the coordinator's turns and 22 of 27 subagents on keeping the
record. Most of that was review: eight failed rounds in S2, largely on steps the
record did not need.

The coordinator's context did not shrink. At turn 70 it held 249k tokens in S1
and 220k in S2, against 201k and 188k for K at the same turn; by then it had sent
60–75k characters of prompts to subagents, against 1–8k in K. Packets built by
pasting `engr show` carry every Section into the coordinator's context as well
as the worker's.

## What went wrong, and why

1. **The two decisions whose reviews were exhausted were lost.** Each became a
   Human candidate on the Object the loop kept admitting to. There is "one live
   Challenge per object" (the protocol), so preparing the second voided the
   first; and a candidate is dead once its Object moves, so the next ChangeSet
   killed the second. The first decision then survived only as a sidecar
   clause, which S3 dropped when it rewrote the summary; its wording is in a
   commit message and nowhere else. A person who comes back to answer finds a
   dead code, or none.
2. **Workers drafted code-level detail as decisions.** "Anything a reader of the
   diff would ask 'why this way?' about" admitted `Option<&str>`, `pub(crate)`
   and exit-code choices, which the Rule's "restates §N" test kept failing; the
   two exhaustions were both such details.
3. **A review saw hand-picked Sections, and the record now contradicts itself.**
   §1 puts interaction events in `uiview.rs`; §40 gives them their own module,
   as the code does. The reviewer of §40 was shown four Sections, not §1.
4. **Done conditions lived in packets** (above).
5. **Items 5 and 6 point at the wrong commit.** `item commit --commit HEAD` ran
   while HEAD was the coordinator's own "delegate item 5" commit.

## What this does not show

- **One run.** Earlier runs of one configuration went opposite ways; the first
  row's difference is far outside that spread, the others are not.
- **A fair comparison of cost or context.** S2 ran twice its cap; per turn, the
  coordinator's context was still larger than K's.
- **Whether the probes were helped.** They were accurate after every cut here,
  as in K; neither recovered the lost decision.

## What would follow

Not yet made, each tied to a failure above:

- An exhausted step goes to the backlog, whole, as a point awaiting a person,
  rather than only to a Human candidate the loop will void. Whether engr should
  keep more than one live candidate per Object, or let an unrelated admission
  leave one alive, is the protocol question this raises.
- Narrow the worker's decision test to choices a later item or a later reader of
  the record needs, not a reader of the diff.
- Give reviewers the whole Object, as `changeset show` renders it.
- Put the check in the item: a short reference in the item text to a Section or
  backlog point that holds it, or a check short enough to fit.
- Let the packet name the engr commands for the worker to run, instead of
  carrying their output through the coordinator's context.
- Close an item with the worker's commit from its report, not `HEAD`.

## Arm M2: the same loop with those answered

The six changes went into `engr-delegate` at `9406549`, and M2 ran with
everything else as M — same prompts, same bar, written down again before it
started — except the runner, which now held the cap across restarts (S1 was cut
at turn 72, S2 at 71; S3 stopped on its own at 70). Claude Code was 2.1.285.
The blind audit is `appendix-m-delegate-rerun-audit.md` (label N): backlog 3,
work items 3, reasoning 2, resume 4.

| | K | M | M2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Decisions in engr within the session that made them | 0 of 19 | 41 of about 49 | about 27 of about 34 (22 as Sections) |
| Items with a check in engr | 0 of 8 | 0 of 6 | 2 of 7 |
| Sidecar matching the repository at a cut | 0 of 2 | both, but for a stale code | S1, its summary saying what item 2's state did not; S2 one step behind |
| Items closed by the end | all | all (S2 past its cap) | 3 of 7 |
| Coordinator context at turn 70, S1 / S2 | 201k / 188k | 249k / 220k | 194k / 140k |
| Cost | US$10.8 | 24.2 | about 15.9 |

**M2 did not meet the bar either**: its items still did not carry their checks.
The coordinator wrote them into each packet again, in its own words, beside the
commands it was now told to name; S3 was the first session to rewrite two items
with a "done when". A rule in the skill about where the check goes did not move
where it went, in two runs out of two.

What the changes did:

- **Naming the commands instead of pasting their output** cut the prompts the
  coordinator sent by turn 70 from 60–75k characters to 5–17k, and its context
  with them.
- **The exhausted decision was kept.** It went to the backlog before the Human
  candidate, the candidate died as before when the next ChangeSet moved the
  Object, and the backlog point still held the question. The sidecar went on
  calling the dead candidate "pending" to the end.
- **The narrower decision test** did not stop the reviews: every S1 round
  failed a step, and S2 spent 52 turns and five subagents admitting two
  Sections from one 122-line change.

What it showed that M did not:

- **A decision whose wording failed review was deleted, not kept.** Eight never
  reached engr, among them that `ui.update` patches a private copy and persists
  only if the result is valid. Each was lost at the same point: the review
  rejected the wording, and the step was removed to let the rest pass. The skill
  says a failed step is fixed; it does not say a removed one must go somewhere.
- **A worker wrote the sidecar.** The packet told it to read the `engr` skill,
  whose rule is to close an item in the same step as its proof, and the worker
  followed that over the packet's "run no engr command that writes, except
  `engr changeset add`". Two skills gave one reader two instructions.
- **The bookkeeping took over.** About 85% of the coordinator's 240 turns went
  to engr, 13 review and cold-read subagents against 3 workers, and the work
  stopped three items short. The Rule's review, not the delegation, is where the
  turns went.

## Across M and M2

Delegation moved the thing no guide wording had moved: decisions reach engr in
the session that makes them, drafted by the agent that made them, in both runs
and by a wide margin. A resuming session and the probes found the true position
within a few turns after every cut. Two things did not move in either run: the
check stayed out of the item, and the review of each recorded decision cost
more turns than the work that produced it.
